title: "What A DJ Actually Carries To Every Event"
date: "2026-07-30"
description: "The full kit is heavier and more specific than most clients imagine. Every item has a reason — and the ones people question most are usually the most important."
---

When I arrive at a venue, there are typically two or three cases plus a bag. People often ask what's in them. The short answer is: everything I need, plus backups of the things that matter most. The longer answer is a specific list, and each item on it is there for a reason.

## The drives

At minimum, two USB drives with the complete library. Identical content, same folder structure, same cue points. If one fails, the other is ready within fifteen seconds. I've never had both fail simultaneously. I don't intend to find out what that looks like.

Beyond the two main drives, I carry a third with a curated emergency selection — not the full library, but the tracks I've played most reliably and know will work in a variety of situations. If everything else fails and I need to make one decision, that drive has the answer.

## The cables

XLR cables in two lengths: short (1.5m) for connecting to house mixers, long (5m) for runs where the DJ booth is not adjacent to the speaker system. Two of each. XLR connectors fail — the locking mechanism wears, pins bend, the cable itself develops an intermittent break from repetitive coiling. Having a spare means the failure costs thirty seconds, not thirty minutes of silent panic.

RCA cables for venues with older equipment. USB-A and USB-B cables in multiple lengths. Ethernet cable, because some venues have network-dependent systems and the wireless signal in a basement ballroom with 300 guests is not reliable. A basic USB hub, because the CDJ-3000 has two USB ports and there are moments when a third slot matters.

Power extension with surge protection. Not because I don't trust the venue's power, but because professional equipment with digital components deserves clean power. A dirty power supply produces noise in the audio chain that's audible at low levels and can corrupt data in worse cases.

## The headphones

Professional monitoring headphones, closed-back, with a coiled cable. Two pairs. The secondary pair isn't used constantly — it stays in the bag — but at an event where headphones are the primary cue monitoring tool, losing them mid-set is catastrophic. The replacement pair has saved at least two events I can think of directly.

The coiled cable matters: it retracts, reduces tangling, and doesn't drape across the mixer in ways that catch on things. An open-back pair for different monitoring situations where isolation isn't needed.

## The laptop

A MacBook Pro with the current library, rekordbox installed and authorised, Serato available as a secondary option. Battery fully charged before every event. Not because I expect to DJ from the laptop — the CDJ-3000 setup doesn't require it — but because rekordbox on the laptop is the preparation tool, the backup display, and the emergency playback option if hardware fails catastrophically.

An external hard drive connected for the event itself, mirroring the USB content, so if the drives fail the laptop can serve as a playback source directly.

## The personal mixer

A compact DJM — not the full two-channel club mixer but a portable unit — for venues where the house equipment is unavailable or unsuitable. This adds weight and complexity to the setup and I don't deploy it at every event. But at corporate events in non-standard venues, hotel ballrooms with aging in-house systems, or outdoor events where no house equipment exists, it's what makes professional-quality mixing possible.

The personal mixer also means I can run a completely independent signal chain if the house system has issues that affect the audio output. It separates my equipment decisions from the venue's infrastructure entirely.

## The adapters

This is the category most people underestimate. A professional DJ's adapter collection includes: XLR to RCA (in both directions), 6.3mm to 3.5mm jack, 3.5mm to dual RCA, TRS to XLR, ground lift adapters for when there's a hum caused by ground loop issues, and a DI box for managing impedance mismatches between different parts of the signal chain.

Each of these adapters exists because a real situation required it. The venues vary enormously in what they have and how it's configured. Without the right adapter, the right cable becomes useless.

## Why the weight is worth it

The full kit weighs enough to matter. Loading in and out requires multiple trips. In venues without lifts, it's a physical commitment. There are events where I've carried equipment up four flights of stairs and wondered why.

The answer is always the same by the end of the night. The music was what it was supposed to be. Nothing stopped. When the house system had an issue, I had a solution. When the venue's USB port wasn't reading the drive, I had a different drive. When the headphone cable failed mid-set, the backup was out and on in under a minute.

That reliability — not the carrying, which is just logistics — is what a professional brings that an underprepared DJ doesn't.
